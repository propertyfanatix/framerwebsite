HDFC Buying Questions, Answered

What is an HDFC income cap?
An HDFC income cap is the maximum household income a buyer can earn to qualify for a Housing Development Fund Corporation (HDFC) co-op in New York City. Caps are set as a percentage of Area Median Income (AMI) and vary by household size and by building, typically ranging from 80% to 165% AMI depending on the specific HDFC's regulatory agreement.
How much do I need for a 20% down payment on an HDFC co-op?
Most HDFC co-ops require a down payment between 10% and 20% of the purchase price, paid in liquid funds such as cash or savings. The exact figure depends on the building's board requirements and financing terms. Confirm the exact down payment percentage with the specific HDFC building before applying.
How do I know if I qualify for an HDFC co-op in Brooklyn or Long Island City?
Qualifying depends on two main factors: household income relative to the building's income cap (based on household size and AMI tier), and available liquid funds for the down payment, typically 10-20% of the purchase price. This free HDFC readiness calculator checks both factors against real building-specific limits in about two minutes.
What happens if my income is over the HDFC cap?
If a buyer's household income exceeds a specific HDFC building's cap by a meaningful margin, that building generally isn't a fit, and it's worth looking at conventional co-ops, condos, or HDFC buildings with higher AMI tiers. A buyer's agent can help reassess mortgage options and identify buildings suited to actual income level.
